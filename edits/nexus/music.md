# Nexus: music brief

## 1. What kind of video this is

| | Measured / observed |
|---|---|
| **Format** | Short vertical talking-head **program-launch promo**: a business/education offer (a creator-economy program with 3 tiers). |
| **Length / pace** | 28.8 s; **206 words per minute** (fast, dense speech); a visual change every 1.3–3 s. |
| **Tone** | Confident, aspirational, "premium tech": dark room, purple backlight, cyan/blue neon UI, money figures climbing to **$10M / month**. |
| **Story arc** | Hook ("welcome to Nexus") → what it is → **3 tiers, each bigger** (Launchpad → Pro → Mastery) → climax ($10M). |
| **Beat grid** | The 5 big hits (2.7 s Nexus, 11.04 Launchpad, 16.0 Media Pro, 21.48 Mastery, 27.32 $10M) all fall on a **~122 BPM** grid (mean error 0.03 s). |

**Category:** *business / luxury-tech promo* (the "high-ticket offer" reel style).

## 2. Music direction

**Best fit: dark modern trap / "luxury business" hip-hop instrumental, 120–124 BPM** (half-time feel, 808 sub, airy plucks or bell synths, crisp hats).
- It matches the dark-neon look and the confident tone.
- Half-time drums leave room for fast speech.
- A 120 BPM track only needs a ~1.7 % stretch to land on the 122 BPM grid.

**Alternatives**
1. **Dark tech / synth pulse, 120 BPM:** "corporate tech" but moody, not cheerful. Cleaner and more "SaaS".
2. **Cinematic hybrid trap:** bigger and more dramatic; good if you want the $10M ending to feel like a trailer.

**Avoid**
- vocals or rap (they fight the voiceover at 206 wpm);
- bright ukulele or "happy corporate" tracks;
- lo-fi (too sleepy);
- EDM with busy mid-range leads (it masks speech).

**Arrangement that suits this edit**

| Time | Music |
|---|---|
| 0–2.7 s | pads / plucks only |
| 2.7 s (NEXUS) | drums enter |
| 11 s (Launchpad) | full beat |
| 21.5 s (Mastery) | adds a riser layer |
| 26.1–27.3 s | ~1 bar drop-out (silence before the hit) |
| 27.32 s ($10M) | full hit |
| end | ring-out into the fade |

## 4. How it will be mixed once you send a track

- Stretch the track to **122 BPM** and line up its downbeats with the 5 big hits.
- Arrange it as in the table in section 2 (pads first, drums at NEXUS, a drop-out before $10M).
- Keep the music under the voice: about −20 dB while you speak, rising to about −14 dB in the gaps.
- Cut low-mids around 300 Hz to 2 kHz so the music doesn't cover your voice.
- Keep the current sound effects on top, then limit the final mix to a −2 dBFS peak.
